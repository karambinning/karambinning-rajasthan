The restaurant

Rajasthan sits on the curve of Railway Road, a few minutes from the station and the South Gate. The room is comfortable, the alcohol licence is in order, and the kitchen runs to a steady tempo six nights a week.

The cooking is broadly North Indian and Bangladeshi — the kind of menu King's Lynn knows well, but treated with patience. Marinades are given the hours they need; spices are toasted rather than thrown in; the curry-base gravies that anchor a Balti or a Rogan are built fresh.

Vegetarians and vegans are looked after — not as a footnote but as a proper section of the menu — and the dining room is happy with anything from a single Rogan and a peshwari naan to a full banquet for ten.

A few dishes the kitchen is known for.

The menu runs long, but these are the plates regulars come back for — and the ones we'd point a first-timer towards.

Chef's pick

Bengal Special

Tender meat in a layered, slightly sweet Bengal-style gravy — best with a piece of garlic naan to lift the sauce.

House classic

Chicken Rogan

The kitchen's everyday yardstick — gentle heat, tomato depth, the kind of plate we'd happily eat off-shift.

For the mild side

Chicken Makhani

Rich and rounded with butter and cream, the temperature dialled down so the spices can sing without shouting.

Garlic notes

Garlic Lamb Balti

Slow-cooked lamb in a fragrant Balti base with a generous hand of garlic — comfort food, dressed for a Saturday.

From the sides

Brinjal Bhaji

Aubergine softened in oil, tempered with cumin and a whisper of fennel — a quiet side that ends up doing the loudest work.

From the tandoor

Garlic Naan · Peshwari Naan

Naan pulled straight from the tandoor — garlic-brushed or stuffed with coconut, almond and a touch of sweetness.
